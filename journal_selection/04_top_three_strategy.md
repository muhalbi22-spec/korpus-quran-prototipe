# 04 · Top-three strategy

Prepared 28 October 2026. These are recommendations, not facts. Nothing here predicts or promises acceptance.

## Positioning decision

Position the article primarily as **Translation Studies**: descriptive, corpus-informed study of sacred-text translation. Corpus linguistics and semantics come second.

1. **The object is a translation layer.** The QAC word-by-word gloss is a documentary, interlinear translation (Nida's "gloss translation"; Nord's documentary translation). Its regularities are translation norms in Toury's sense.
2. **Corpus linguistics journals** would expect methodological novelty: retrieval, annotation or statistics. The study uses an existing annotated resource descriptively.
3. **Semantics journals** would expect a contribution to lexical-semantic theory. The article applies Baker and Izutsu rather than extending them.
4. **Religious-studies journals** (JQS, Al-Jāmiʿah) are viable backups. Their reviewers will assess the tafsīr argument more than the translation theory, which is the weaker side of the manuscript.

## Recommended top three

### 1. First choice: *Perspectives: Studies in Translation Theory and Practice* (Taylor & Francis)

**Why first:**
- Its stated remit is the meeting point of translation theory and practice. The revised article fits that remit directly: a documentary gloss layer judged against four sentence-level translations, with practical recommendations for layered glossing.
- About 8,000 words fits the revised length (~7,500 including references).
- Q1 in Linguistics & Language (V3). The publisher page shows a Q1 CiteScore and SJR 2025 = 0.935 (V2).
- The subscription route is expected to be free (T&F hybrid model).

**Conditions before submitting (10 minutes, see `06`):**
- Confirm on SCImago that *Perspectives* is Q1 in 2025.
- Confirm on its "Instructions for authors" that publishing without OA carries no APC and that its GenAI policy allows the AI assistance you used.
- If either check fails, **Hikma becomes the first choice.**

**What must change for this journal:**
- Lead with the theoretical claim: interlinear glossing as documentary translation, and default-gloss narrowing as a norm.
- Bring the corpus distribution in as evidence for that claim, rather than presenting it first.
- Keep tafsīr detail concise.
- Add a "practical implications" section on glossing design.
- Use the T&F reference style required in the instructions; the draft uses author–date.

### 2. Second choice: *Hikma: Revista de Traducción* (Universidad de Córdoba)

**Timing:** the next submission window is **15 January – 15 February 2027**, then 15 September – 15 October 2027. If *Perspectives* desk-rejects before mid-January, Hikma's window follows without overlap.

**What must change:**

### 3. Third choice: *The Translator* (Taylor & Francis)

**Why third:**
- Strong fit with translation as cultural and ideological mediation. The kāfir analysis (9:123, reception risk) and the comparison of translators' strategies suit it.
- The no-APC subscription route is confirmed at V2.
- Its 2025 quartile is not yet confirmed: Q1 L&L 2024 (V3), SJR 2025 = 0.633 on the publisher page.

**What must change:**
- Strengthen the ideology/reception thread: Venuti; recent work on ideology in Qur'an translation (Gunawan 2022; Suaidi & Arifin 2021; Erebih & Almanna 2026; Aldeeb 2026).
- Shorten the descriptive statistics.
- Check the word limit in the instructions.

## Desk-rejection risks (all targets)

1. **Interlinear-gloss problem.** A reviewer may argue that judging a gloss layer for theological adequacy is a category error. The revision addresses this head-on and must not be watered down.
2. **AI disclosure.** Substantial AI drafting must be disclosed. Some journals limit AI to language polishing (New Voices, JoSTrans). The author must own and rewrite the text.
3. **Single-coder subjectivity** in the formal/dynamic and Baker coding. Mitigation: explicit criteria, published token table, second coder.
4. **Scope:**
   - JoSTrans (non-literary only): high risk.
   - JQS / Al-Jāmiʿah (religious studies): medium.
   - Perspectives / The Translator / Hikma: low.
5. **Theological normativity.** Phrases like "theologically thin" or "the correct meaning" read as confessional. The revision frames meanings as positions documented in named tafsīr.
6. **Sensitive verse (9:123).** Keep the claims sourced (al-Ṭabarī) and hedged; avoid political commentary.
7. **Data errors** (verse mislabels, mismatched quotations). These are corrected in the revision; recheck before submission.
8. **Weak references** (Zenodo deposits, possibly discontinued journals). These are removed or flagged in the revision.
