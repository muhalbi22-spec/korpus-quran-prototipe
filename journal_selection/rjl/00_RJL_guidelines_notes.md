# Russian Journal of Linguistics: author guidelines (read 1 Nov 2026)

Sources: https://journals.rudn.ru/linguistics/about/submissions , …/about/editorialPolicies , sample article https://journals.rudn.ru/linguistics/article/view/52498 (Vol 30 No 3, 2026).
V1 = read on the official page. "Not stated" = the guidelines do not say.

## Verified (V1)
- Fees: none ("Publication process in this journal is free for all authors"; "no article processing charge").
- Indexing claimed by the journal: Scopus, WoS Core Collection (ESCI), DOAJ, etc. **Quartile: not stated by the journal and NOT yet verified on SCImago**; the author must check scimagojr.com before submitting.
- Scope includes "language and culture … theory and practice of translation"; sections on cultural semantics.
- Double-blind review; aim 2–5 months; revisions within 2 months; max three rounds. CC BY-NC 4.0.
- Length: original study ≤ **7000–9000 words including tables and references**.
- Word file (.doc/.docx/.rtf); **Times New Roman 12 pt, 1.5 line spacing, 2 cm margins**; no underlining (italic/bold OK).
- File structure (one file): title → authors + affiliations (superscript numbers) on the opening page → abstract → keywords → body (Introduction … materials, methods, results, Discussion, Conclusions) → Acknowledgements (conflict of interest; funding; optional roles/thanks) → References. Tables numbered with headings (sample article: caption **above** the table: "Table 1" then title). Figures: separate 300 dpi files.
- Abstract: **200–250 words**; background + AIMS, MATERIALS & METHODS, RESULTS, closing CONCLUSIONS sentence (one unstructured paragraph in the sample).
- Keywords: guidelines "5 to 6 keywords are preferable" (web form allows 3–10).
- In-text citation: "(Leech 2014) (Leech 2014: 105) (Bousfield & Locher 2008) (Hisakata, Nishida & Johnston 2016) (Davies et al. 2013: 275)"; narrative "Myers (1990: 42)", "Kim and Lu (2024)".
- References (Unified Style for Linguistics journals): alphabetical then chronological; only works cited; DOI encouraged; 25–50 recommended. Lexical-word capitalisation for journal and book titles; sentence case for article/chapter titles. Examples:
  - Leech, Geoffrey N. 2014. *The Pragmatics of Politeness*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.
  - Kecskes, Istvan. 2007. Formulaic language in English lingua franca. In Istvan Kecskes & Laurence R. Horn (eds.), *Explorations in pragmatics: Linguistic, cognitive and intercultural aspects*, 191–219. Berlin/New York: Mouton de Gruyter.
  - House, Juliane. 2006. Communicative styles in English and German. *European Journal of English Studies* 10 (3). 249–267.
  - Haugh, Michael, Dániel Kádár & Sara Mills. 2013. Interpersonal pragmatics: Issues and debates. *Journal of Pragmatics* 58. 1–11. DOI: 10.1016/j.pragma.2013.09.009
  - Website: Native Languages of the Americas. 1998–2014. Vocabulary in Native American languages: Salish words. http://www.native-languages.org/salish_words.htm (accessed 25 February 2018).
  (The web-form text also says "Harvard guidelines in APA Style": the page contradicts itself; we follow the detailed Unified Style examples.)
- Bionote: 70–100 words: first name, surname, academic degree, position, department, affiliation, research interests, e-mail.
- Cover letter: scanned **PDF signed** by all authors; says concisely why the paper is a valuable addition and relates it to previous work.
- AI policy (editorialPolicies): "a clear statement on the use of AI tools (including which tools were used and for what purpose)" and "reproducible materials (corpus inventory, link to the used materials, codebook, intermediate tables …)", which "can be provided as Appendices".
- Simultaneous submission is forbidden. Self-archiving allowed.

## Not stated
Separate title page / anonymised file (guidelines put names on the opening page); footnote policy; transliteration rules; how to cite translated/classical works; ORCID requirement.
