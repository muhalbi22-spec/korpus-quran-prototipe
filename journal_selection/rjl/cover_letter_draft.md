We are pleased to submit the manuscript "Between gloss and faith: Interlinear glossing, sentence-level translation and the semantic narrowing of *taqwā*, *raḥmat* and *kāfir* in the Quranic Arabic Corpus" for consideration as an original research article in the *Russian Journal of Linguistics*.

The article addresses a question in the theory and practice of translation and in lexical semantics: where meaning is narrowed when key religious terms are rendered into English. Corpus-based studies of Qur'an translation often treat the English shown in annotated resources as "the translation" of a term. We argue that such resources contain layers with different functions, and we show this with every token of three lemmas in the Quranic Arabic Corpus (158 tokens, plus 131 participial tokens of the *kāfir* pattern), comparing the corpus's interlinear gloss with five sentence-level English translations displayed on the same platform.

The study relates to previous work in three ways. It revisits Nida's equivalence framework, in particular his notion of gloss translation, and reads the interlinear gloss through Toury's translational norms and Nord's documentary translation. It uses Baker's typology of lexical meaning together with Izutsu's distinction between basic and relational meaning in the Qur'an to separate word-level meaning from relational and exegetical knowledge. And it extends corpus-based research on Qur'an translation, which has mostly analysed selected verses or single *sūrahs*, by reporting full lemma distributions with token-level traceability. We believe the resulting distinction between three loci of narrowing, and the methodological safeguards proposed for corpus-based research on sacred-text translation, will interest the journal's readers in translation studies and language and culture.

We confirm that the manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration by any other journal. The article develops data first compiled for the corresponding author's Master's thesis (Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta, 2025); the dataset was re-verified against the live corpus and re-coded, and the comparison with five sentence-level translations and the norm-based, documentary-translation framework go beyond the thesis. The token-level coding table, with a codebook, is provided as Supplementary File 1 to support replication, and the manuscript includes a statement on the use of generative AI tools. Both authors have approved the submission, and we declare no conflict of interest.
